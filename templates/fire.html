Fire & smoke

Every camera is looked at about once a minute, day and night, by a model trained only for fire and smoke. To keep false alarms away it alerts only when the fire or smoke is seen 3 times at the same spot and it flickers or drifts - an orange lamp, a red shirt or sunlight on a wall stays the same and never alerts. Then you get a photo, a loud note on every page and a phone alert.

Fire and smoke rules

Runs all day and all night. It uses about a third of the detection's time, so people counting gets a little slower.

Off: only flames alert. Smoke shows a fire earlier, but steam, dust and fog can look like it.

Whatever this is, the fire still has to be seen 3 times at one spot and has to flicker.

Untick a camera to leave it out - one that sees a kitchen stove or a candle, say. To leave out only part of a picture (a TV, a window with sunset) mark it with Ignore areas on the camera's live view.

A spot is learnt when the model kept calling something there fire but it never flickered, or when you press Not fire on a record. It alerts there again only if the model is very sure.

Test the model with a photo

Pick a photo or a video still of a fire (or of something you think could be mistaken for one). It shows what the model finds and what the checks say. On a camera, the 3-times and flicker checks come on top - a photo cannot show those. To test a camera itself, play a fire video on a phone in front of it for half a minute.
